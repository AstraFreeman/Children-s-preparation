This dissertation addressed the scientific problem of developing a methodology for teaching
digital history to future professionals in higher education.
The following main results were obtained.

\begin{enumerate}

\item A PRISMA-guided systematic review of empirical pedagogical studies on digital history
identified only 6 studies from 46 Scopus records~\cite{Korniienko2025cte}, all reporting
positive engagement effects yet none documenting wartime conditions or multi-tool resilience
architectures. The scarcity of rigorous empirical work confirms that digital history pedagogy
remains a nascent research field requiring dedicated methodological development.

\item A systematic review of 74 gamification studies in history education~\cite{KorniienkoSemerikov2024}
revealed severe definitional ambiguity (14.9\% providing explicit definitions), predominantly
positive but uncertain effects on engagement (70.3\%) and learning ($d\,{=}\,0.28$--$0.61$),
and six evidence-based design principles. The four-type taxonomy of digital history approaches
developed from this synthesis provides a conceptual framework for positioning future
interventions within the broader field.

\item Methodological quality assessment using the MRQS framework~\cite{Korniienko2025mrqs}
demonstrated that 83.8\% of the gamification corpus exhibited high risk of bias. LLM-assisted
validation established that automated risk-of-bias scoring achieves only slight agreement
($\kappa\,{=}\,-0.04$ to $0.21$), while factual data extraction is reliable (82--97\%),
defining the boundary between automatable and expert-dependent research synthesis tasks.

\item Eight implementation guidelines for gamification in history education were developed,
aligned with all five DigComp~2.0 competence areas~\cite{Vuorikari2016, Titova2025cte}
and accompanied by a 12-item CONSORT/PRISMA-aligned reporting
checklist~\cite{Schulz2010CONSORT, Page2021}. Together, these address both the design
gap and the transparency gap documented in the systematic reviews.

\item A seven-month pedagogical experiment at the CDPO of Kryvyi Rih State Pedagogical
University (October~2024 -- May~2025) served 23~students across 40~lectures, 40~seminars
with 156~Google Forms quizzes, and 7~modular controls~\cite{Korniienko2025aredu}. Three
enrollment waves confirmed the open-network connectivist model~\cite{Siemens2004} under
authentic wartime distance-learning conditions.

\item A six-module Python data analysis pipeline was developed, enabling deterministic
processing of chat logs, assessment data, and platform catalogues. The pipeline directly
addresses the reproducibility deficit documented by the MRQS audit~\cite{Korniienko2025mrqs}.

\item The HistoryLikeGame platform evolved through an iterative DBR
trajectory~\cite{Reeves2003, Korniienko2024hlg}: v1 (December~2024: 23~quiz modules,
205~KB, unchanged throughout the experiment) $\to$ v2 (post-experiment: 62~modules,
13~game types, 9~historical periods, 10~AR scenes). The platform's offline-capable
architecture is directly transferable to conflict-affected and low-bandwidth educational
environments.

\item Longitudinal assessment across 36~seminars and 7~modular controls documented
topic-specificity effects (single-period peak: 94.2\% vs.\ comprehensive NMT-format:
33.3\%) and a U-shaped modular control arc. Four trajectory clusters (consistent mastery,
topic-variable, persistent struggle, progressive decline) confirmed that gamification
did not reduce performance heterogeneity~\cite{SailerHomner2020, BaiHewHuang2020}.

\item The five-layer wartime resilience architecture demonstrated that asynchronous-first,
minimal-bandwidth design maintained assessment continuity despite 33~documented absences
(57.5\% from external causes). Stable modular control participation ($N\,{=}\,7$--$27$
across 7~months) and an engagement floor of 73.9\% confirm that no single infrastructure
failure blocked educational access~\cite{Burde2017, UNESCO2023}.

\item The course simultaneously instantiated all four digital history approach types and
anchored all five DigComp~2.0 competence areas~\cite{Vuorikari2016}, defining a
\emph{digital history methodology model}: a multi-approach, theoretically grounded,
infrastructure-adaptive system that treats digital tools as the primary medium for
historical learning, assessment, and community~\cite{Ertmer1999, Humeniuk2022113}.

\item The concept of \textit{reward-bridged learning} (RBL) was proposed as a distinct
position on the game integration spectrum~\cite{RBL2025}, where educational micro-tasks
are embedded as optional pathways within entertainment games. Unlike tangential
learning~\cite{frith2021} and stealth learning~\cite{ShuteFormative2008}, RBL is explicit,
voluntary, and reward-driven, extending \citeauthor{Deterding2011}'s~\cite{Deterding2011}
taxonomy by reversing the directionality of game--education integration.

\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Hypothesis status.}
The research hypothesis predicted that gamification-enhanced digital history teaching
would improve engagement and learning outcomes. The empirical evidence provides partial
support: behavioural engagement was documented through platform usage and sustained
participation despite wartime disruptions, and the engagement floor of 73.9\% confirms
cohort retention. However, the format-controlled pre--post learning outcome gap
(1.6\,pp) falls at the lower bound of the field's $d\,{=}\,0.28$--$0.61$ distribution,
and three confounding factors~-- declining participation, increasing topic difficulty,
and data-format transition~-- preclude causal attribution. The hypothesis is therefore
partially supported for engagement but remains empirically unresolved for learning outcomes
under the present single-group design.

\textbf{Future research directions.}
The detailed three-tier future research agenda is presented in Section~\ref{sec:future-research} of
Chapter~\ref{ch:results}. The most consequential unresolved question~-- whether the post-gamification
participation decline reflects modality substitution or disengagement~-- requires
privacy-preserving learning analytics in a future HistoryLikeGame iteration. A
quasi-experimental controlled comparison across parallel CDPO sections would enable the
causal inference that the present design cannot provide.

The results of the dissertation have been implemented in the educational process of the
Centre for Pre-University Training and Education of Kryvyi Rih State Pedagogical University.
