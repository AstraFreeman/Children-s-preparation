\conc{

This dissertation addressed the scientific problem of developing a methodology for teaching
digital history to future professionals.
The following main results were obtained.

\begin{enumerate}

\item A PRISMA-guided systematic review of empirical pedagogical studies on digital history
was conducted, identifying only 6 studies meeting rigorous inclusion criteria from an initial
pool of 46 Scopus records \cite{Korniienko2025cte}.
All 6 studies reported positive engagement effects (100\%), but none operated under wartime
conditions, none documented a multi-tool resilience architecture, and none spanned more than
a single semester of iterative implementation.
This finding establishes the nascent state of digital history pedagogy as a research field.

\item A comprehensive systematic review of gamification in history education was performed,
synthesising evidence from 74 studies \cite{KorniienkoSemerikov2024}.
The review revealed: (a)~severe definitional ambiguity, with only 14.9\% of studies providing
an explicit definition of gamification \cite{Deterding2011};
(b)~predominantly positive effects on engagement (70.3\%) and learning outcomes (51.4\%;
$d\,{=}\,0.28$--$0.61$, concentrated on factual recall), consistent with broader gamification
meta-analyses \cite{SailerHomner2020, BaiHewHuang2020};
(c)~six evidence-based design principles --- historical authenticity, narrative integration,
progressive difficulty, immediate feedback, social elements, and multiple modalities;
(d)~a four-type taxonomy of digital history approaches (archival-analytical,
creative-productive, collaborative-communicative, immersive-experiential).

\item A methodological quality assessment of the 74 gamification studies was conducted using
the MRQS framework \cite{Korniienko2025mrqs}, revealing that 83.8\% exhibited high risk of
bias, with a mean MRQS of 4.31/10 (SD\,=\,1.93, range 0--9).
An LLM validation approach demonstrated that automated risk-of-bias scoring achieves only
slight inter-rater agreement ($\kappa\,{=}\,-0.04$ to $0.21$ per the \citealt{LandisKoch1977}
scale), while factual data extraction achieves high reliability (82--97\%), establishing
a hybrid human--LLM model for educational research synthesis \cite{TamimMMRQG2021, Page2021}.

\item Implementation guidelines for gamification in history education were developed based
on the synthesis of both systematic reviews and aligned with all five areas of the European
Digital Competence Framework (DigComp~2.0) \cite{Vuorikari2016, Titova2025cte}.
Eight evidence-grounded guidelines operationalise the six design principles through
discipline-specific DigComp adaptations --- the first DigComp-2.0-aligned implementation
framework in the history-education gamification literature.
A 12-item reporting checklist aligned with CONSORT/PRISMA standards \cite{Schulz2010CONSORT,
Page2021} addresses the field's documented transparency gap.

\item A seven-month pedagogical experiment was designed and implemented at the Centre for
Pre-University Training and Education of Kryvyi Rih State Pedagogical University
(October~2024 -- May~2025), serving 23~students across 40~lectures, 40~seminars with
156~Google Forms quizzes, and 7~modular controls \cite{Korniienko2025aredu}.
Three distinct enrollment waves (October, November, January--April) confirmed
\citeauthor{Siemens2004}'s open-network connectivist model~\cite{Siemens2004} in practice.

\item A Python-based data analysis pipeline was developed comprising 6 modules (chat log
parsing, assessment timeline extraction, score analysis, attendance tracking, gamification
cataloguing, and figure generation), enabling deterministic and reproducible processing of
all collected data.
This directly addresses the reproducibility gap identified by the MRQS audit, where 90.5\%
of the 74 reviewed studies provided insufficient replication detail \cite{Korniienko2025mrqs}.

\item The HistoryLikeGame interactive gamification platform was developed through an iterative
design-based research trajectory \cite{Reeves2003, Korniienko2024hlg}:
v1 (December~2024: 23~quiz modules, 205~KB) $\to$ v2 (current: 62 modules, 13~game types,
9~historical periods, 10~A-Frame AR scenes, $\approx$869~KB total with $\approx$88\,KB shared
infrastructure).
This constitutes the first documented practitioner DBR cycle in the digital history pedagogy
literature, and the platform's offline-capable lightweight architecture is a directly
transferable design principle for conflict-affected and low-bandwidth educational environments.

\item Longitudinal assessment (36 seminars, 7 modular controls) documented a topic-specificity
pattern: single-period assessments peaked at 94.2\% (Seminar~13) versus 33.3\% for
comprehensive NMT-format assessments (Seminar~30).
Modular controls traced a U-shaped arc (MC2: 84.6\% $\to$ MC5: 63.8\% $\to$ MC7: 74.3\%),
with a format-controlled pre--post gap of 1.6\,pp at the lower bound of the field's
$d\,{=}\,0.28$--$0.61$ distribution.
Four persistent trajectory clusters were identified (consistent mastery 37.5\%, topic-variable
25\%, persistent struggle 25\%, progressive decline 12.5\%), confirming that gamification
did not reduce performance heterogeneity \cite{SailerHomner2020, BaiHewHuang2020}.

\item The five-layer wartime resilience architecture empirically demonstrated its
effectiveness: 33 documented absences (57.5\% attributable to external causes ---
internet: 24.2\%, family: 24.2\%, power: 9.1\%) coincided with stable modular control
participation ($N\,{=}\,7$--$27$ across all 7 controls spanning 7~months), confirming that
the asynchronous-first, minimal-bandwidth design prevented any single infrastructure failure
from blocking assessment completion \cite{Burde2017, UNESCO2023, Martynets202428,
Zamkova2023299}.
The engagement floor --- 17 of 23 enrolled students (73.9\%) in at least one post-gamification
assessment --- confirms that the core cohort remained connected throughout despite wartime
disruption \cite{Hlianenko2024}.

\item The course simultaneously instantiated all four digital history approach types
(archival-analytical: 156 NMT-format quizzes; collaborative-communicative: 400+ Viber
messages; immersive-experiential: 62 game modules; creative-productive: thematic quiz naming)
and empirically anchored all five DigComp~2.0 competence areas \cite{Vuorikari2016},
defining a \emph{digital history methodology model}: a multi-approach, theoretically grounded
(TPACK, ZPD, SDT, Flow Theory), infrastructure-adaptive system that treats digital tools not
as supplements but as the primary medium through which historical learning, assessment, and
community are delivered \cite{Ertmer1999, Humeniuk2022113, Nunez202329}.

\end{enumerate}

\item The concept of \textit{reward-bridged learning} (RBL) was proposed as a distinct,
previously untheorised position on the game integration spectrum (Figure~\ref{fig:gamification-spectrum}),
occupying the space between game-based learning and serious games where the
directionality of integration is reversed: educational micro-tasks (quizzes, knowledge checks)
are embedded as optional bonus pathways within entertainment games, incentivised through
in-game rewards \cite{RBL2025}. RBL is distinguished from tangential learning \cite{frith2021}
(implicit, self-directed) and stealth learning \cite{ShuteFormative2008} (hidden education) by
being explicit, voluntary, and reward-driven. This extends \citeauthor{Deterding2011}'s~\cite{Deterding2011}
foundational taxonomy by identifying a class of interventions where the \textit{game} is primary
and \textit{education} is the added element --- the conceptual inverse of gamification.

\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Scientific problems addressable by these results.}
The documented digital history methodology model and its empirical validation directly address
three interconnected scientific problems: (a)~the absence of theoretically grounded,
empirically tested frameworks for technology-enhanced history education, particularly in
conflict-affected contexts; (b)~the methodological quality deficit in gamification research
(83.8\% high risk of bias), addressable through the MRQS instrument, LLM validation protocol,
and reporting checklist; (c)~the digital competence development gap in Ukrainian
history education, addressable through the DigComp-aligned implementation guidelines and the
freely accessible HistoryLikeGame platform.

\textbf{Future research directions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Anonymous learning analytics and substitution hypothesis testing}: Adding privacy-preserving, opt-in session analytics to HistoryLikeGame~v3 (anonymised \texttt{localStorage} records with no personal identifiers or background transmission) to confirm or refute whether the post-gamification voluntary seminar decline ($N$: 12.9$\to$5.6) reflects modality substitution or disengagement --- the most consequential unresolved question from the present study \cite{KoivistoHamari2019, Sailer2017}.
\item \textit{Quasi-experimental controlled comparison}: A 2026--2027 implementation
across two parallel CDPO course sections (with and without gamification) to enable causal
inference, satisfying the MRQS criterion M8 currently unmet by 68.9\% of the 74-study
corpus \cite{Schulz2010CONSORT, creswell, KorniienkoSemerikov2024}.
\item \textit{NMT outcome follow-up}: Linking CDPO enrollment records to the May~2025
NMT examination results for the 23~enrolled students --- the first direct downstream
outcome measure in the 74-study corpus \cite{Korniienko2025cte, Page2021}.
\item \textit{Multi-institution scaling}: Expanding to 3--5 CDPO institutions across
different Ukrainian oblasts (frontline, mid-country, western rear) to test the resilience
architecture's generalisability across systematically varying infrastructure disruption
profiles \cite{UNESCO2023, Striuk_2022, Cohen2005, Borenstein2009}.
\item \textit{Phenomenographic study of wartime student experience}: Interview research
with 8--10 consenting former participants to document the psychological and situational
dimensions of gamified distance learning under armed conflict --- inaccessible to
quantitative assessment data \cite{Ditlmann2025, Hamari2014, DichevDicheva2017,
Zamkova2023299, Martynets202428}.
\item \textit{Reward-bridged learning prototype}: Developing and empirically testing an
RBL implementation for HistoryLikeGame~v3, where educational quiz completion unlocks
bonus game content (additional levels, cosmetic rewards, leaderboard advantages) ---
testing the theoretical prediction that entertainment-primary incentive structures
produce higher voluntary engagement than education-primary ones \cite{RBL2025, Sailer2017}.
\end{enumerate}

The results of the dissertation have been implemented in the educational process of the
Centre for Pre-University Training and Education of Kryvyi Rih State Pedagogical University.
